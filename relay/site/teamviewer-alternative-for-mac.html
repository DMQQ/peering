A TeamViewer Alternative for Your Own Mac

An honest comparison, written by the people who make Peering. TeamViewer facts are from TeamViewer's own site, November 2026.

The short version. TeamViewer is built for support teams helping many computers on every platform. If what you actually do is reach your own Mac from your own phone, it is a lot of product with an account and a licence model aimed at businesses. Peering does that one job, with no account and a free tier at home.

Two different jobs

TeamViewer's strengths are real: it reaches Windows, Mac, Linux and mobile devices, it can connect to someone else's computer from an ID and a password they read out, and it has management, deployment and auditing for IT departments. Peering does none of that. It pairs a phone or tablet with a computer you own, once, in person, and is designed around what you do on your own Mac from a phone.

Why people look for an alternative

The common story is someone using the free version to reach their own computer who gets a message that the session looks commercial. Peering has no such check: its free tier is defined by the network, not by what you are doing. On your own Wi-Fi there is no limit; away from it, 30 minutes a day are free, and a paid plan lifts that.

The other is weight. A tool built for fleets has a console, groups, policies and an account to keep secure. Peering's whole setup is installing two apps and confirming six digits.

Where TeamViewer is the better fit

If you support other people, manage computers you have never touched, need Linux, or need audit logs and central deployment, use TeamViewer or something like it. Peering is not trying to be that.

Where Peering is the better fit

  • It is your Mac and your phone, and you do not want an account between them.
  • You work from the phone: Mac keys, trackpad mode, the Mac's sound, files and a clipboard that takes images.
  • You run coding agents and want their chats and approvals on your phone. See Claude Code on Android.
  • You want a price you can predict: free at home, $2.99 a month or $24.99 once for unlimited.

What setting up Peering involves

On the Mac, download Peering and grant the two permissions macOS asks for: Screen Recording and Accessibility. On the phone, install Peering for Android (coming soon to Google Play) or for iPhone. With both on the same Wi-Fi the Mac shows up under Nearby; tap it, compare the six digits and you are done. There is no ID to note down and no password to set, because the two devices already know each other's keys.

From then on the phone reaches the Mac from anywhere. Peering tries a direct path between the devices first and uses its relay only when the networks refuse one. To keep the Mac reachable while you travel, see keeping a Mac awake for remote access.

Security, briefly

Peering's devices make their own keys, keep them in their own secure storage and confirm each other with a code the first time. The relay that carries a session from another network forwards encrypted bytes it holds no key for, and there is no password that a stranger could guess or phish to reach your Mac.

Questions

Is there a free TeamViewer alternative for personal use?

For reaching your own Mac from a phone, Peering is free on your own network with no time limit and includes 30 minutes a day from elsewhere. Pro or Lifetime removes the daily limit.

Does Peering need an account like TeamViewer?

No. There is no account on the Mac or the phone.

Can I use Peering to help a family member?

Only if you can pair with their computer in person first. Peering is not built for ad hoc support sessions, where TeamViewer is the better tool.

Does Peering work on Android?

Yes, the Android app is coming soon to Google Play, for Android 10 or later. An iPhone and iPad app is available now.
