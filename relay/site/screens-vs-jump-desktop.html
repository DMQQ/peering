Screens 5 vs Jump Desktop

Written by the people who make Peering, so read it knowing that. Researched September 2026 from each product's own pages.

The short version. Jump Desktop has its own faster protocol, carries sound and is a one-time purchase. Screens is a polished VNC client with a subscription or a larger one-off price and no sound. If you only own Macs and want no account, Peering is a third way.

Where Jump Desktop is the better fit

You want sound, high frame rates, or to reach Windows with a protocol made for it. It is also the cheaper route over time, since the iOS app is a one-time purchase.

Where Screens is the better fit

You want the most polished Apple-native VNC client, you need Linux, or you want Curtain Mode and temporary assistance sessions for helping others.

If you only have Macs

Both of these are built to reach everything. If the other end is always a Mac, Peering does less and costs less to start: no account, the Mac's sound, and free on your own network with no time limit. Away from home it gives 30 minutes a day free, and $2.99 a month or $24.99 once for unlimited.

Common questions

Is Screens 5 or Jump Desktop better?

Jump Desktop carries the computer's sound, has its own high frame rate protocol and is a one-time purchase per platform. Screens is a polished VNC client with Curtain Mode and reaches anything that runs VNC. For a mixed setup of Mac and Windows, Jump Desktop is usually the stronger fit.

Does Screens 5 support audio?

Screens' own documentation says it does not carry computer sound, because it works over VNC.

Does Jump Desktop work on iPhone?

Yes. Jump Desktop has an iPhone and iPad app, sold as a one-time purchase.
