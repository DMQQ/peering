2. What Peering is

Peering shows your Mac's screen on your iPhone and lets you control it. Your two devices pair directly with each other. When they are on different networks, a relay server operated by the developer of Peering introduces them and forwards their encrypted connection. The relay cannot read what it forwards. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what it handles.

Peering is a tool for reaching your own computers. It is not a remote support or surveillance product, and it is not designed to be installed on a machine without its owner's knowledge or consent.

4. Free use and relay time

Connecting over your own local network is free and is not limited. When your devices are on different networks, the relay is what introduces them and what carries the session, and that is metered: the free allowance is 30 minutes in each 24-hour period, and it does not accumulate. Every paid plan includes unlimited relay time. The picture itself takes a path straight between your two devices when their networks allow one, which does not change what the relay meters. The app shows an estimate of what is left of the allowance you are on, counted from the sessions recorded on that device.

The allowance, its length and the plans available may change. Changes to the free allowance do not apply retroactively to a period already in progress.
