What it does

All of it, listed rather than summarised. Neither end needs an account.

Encrypted, device to device

Each device makes its own key and keeps it in its own Keychain. There is no account and nothing stored on a server.

Locked behind your face

Ask for Face ID every time the app comes back, and a locked phone is the only thing that unlocks the Mac.

An SSH server can carry its own lock too, with a passcode of your own.

Your Macs, named how you like

Rename a Mac to something you recognise, or forget one you are done with, from the row itself.

The list is the pairing. A Mac in it is one the phone can reach; one you forget is one it cannot.

Relay

Every Mac you own

Free pairs with one Mac. Relay pairs with every Mac you own, and they all live in the same list.

Pro

More than one at once

One Mac at a time is free on every plan: starting a session with a second one ends the first.

Pro holds several open together, so moving between two machines is a tap rather than a reconnect.

Come back from anywhere

After pairing, your two devices find each other when one of them is on another network.

Straight between the two

When your networks allow it, the picture takes a path the two devices find for themselves.

Only the control connection goes through the relay.

See which way it went

No guessing about which path you got: the session screen says LAN, RELAY or DIRECT·P2P.

The info panel shows the round trip of the lane the picture is on, counted apart from the control connection.

Says so when the network goes

Change network mid-session and the connection does not fail, it goes quiet: a screen left looking alive while nothing moves.

Peering counts that quiet and ends the session after ten seconds, so you are told rather than left guessing.

You actually drive it

Pointer, keyboard, joystick, scroll wheel and macros. Control & zoom puts your fingers on the Mac.

Observe, or take over

Control mode sends your touches to the Mac. Observe mode shows the screen and sends nothing at all, for when you only want to watch something run.

Zoom, fit, or fill

Pinch to zoom in on a toolbar, or let the Mac's screen fill the phone's and keep the edges you care about in view.

A screen, an app, or one window

Every display the Mac is attached to, one running app, or a single window. Share any of them, and switch between them without reconnecting.

Open an app you forgot to launch

The list is live and includes applications that are installed but not running. Tap one and the Mac opens it, then it is a source like any other.

A picture that gives way rather than stalls

The bitrate follows what the connection can actually carry, stepping down when latency rises and back up when it settles.

Pin it yourself to Low, Medium or High if you would rather decide.

Your servers, too

Add a host over SSH and it sits beside your Macs. Its keys are checked the first time, and it can carry its own lock.

Free on every plan: what a plan runs out of is shells open at once, not machines.

A real shell

Full-screen terminal sessions with a key row built for a phone: escape, control keys, arrows and word-at-a-time movement.

One shell at a time is free on every plan. Named tabs, to keep several open at once, are Relay Plus.

Relay

Files, on either machine

Walk the folders of your Mac or of any server you have added, open a config or a script, change it and save it, make new ones and rename or delete what is listed.

All of that is free. Moving a file between the phone and the machine is Relay.

A picture opens as a picture

What a file is comes from its own bytes rather than from its name: an image is drawn as an image, and an SVG is rendered with scripts off.

Make, rename, delete

A new file or folder, a rename on any row, and a delete that says what it will take with it.

On your own Mac that goes to the Bin; on a server it is gone, and the confirmation says so.

Run what you are looking at

An executable row carries a Run button, confirmed by its name and path.

What it sends is a line typed into a shell you already have, so a script that prints something prints it where you can read it.

Every session, remembered

Each connection is filed with how long it lasted, how it was reached, how much came down the wire, and what the Mac said along the way.

One clipboard

Copy on the iPhone and paste on the Mac, or the other way round, on every plan. Relay keeps the last twelve, so what you copied a while ago is still waiting.

The commands you type every time

A row of chips above the keyboard holding the commands and snippets you reach for, in every terminal. Nothing fires until you tap it.

Running the ones you already have is free on every plan. Making or editing one is Pro.

Macros

Record a sequence once and fire it with one tap: the things you type every time you sit down at that Mac.

Saved on the phone, editable, and never sent anywhere until you run it.

Told when you are needed

A notification when a Mac is waiting on a pairing code, an SSH server is asking for a password, or a shell has stopped for an answer you have not given.

Siri and Shortcuts

Ask Siri to connect to a Mac, open a shell on one of your servers, or end what is still open, without unlocking the phone and finding the app first.

The Mac end is an app too

It sits in the menu bar and can open at login, so a restart does not quietly end your remote access.

It shows the two permissions it needs, each with a button to the right System Settings pane.

Wake it, or hide it

Wake a sleeping Mac over the network before you connect, and put its screen away when you would rather nobody walking past saw it.

Encrypted end to end. The relay is not a party to it.

Your two devices hold the keys. The relay is never given them.

What the relay handles

Your devices' IDs, your Mac's name, the protocol version, which plan the app says you are on, and how much relay time has been used.

It sees the address each device connects from, as any server does, and hands each of them the other's. What it can read is nothing, on either path.

What it cannot read

The control channel is a TLS session belonging to your two devices, copied through without being terminated.

Video, audio, keystrokes, pointer input and clipboard text are sealed with keys the relay was never given.

What the app collects

Nothing. No analytics, no advertising, no third-party tracking SDKs, and no account to attach any of it to.

Questions

Can the relay see my screen or my controls?

No. It forwards the encrypted connection between your paired devices and cannot read the video, audio, clipboard text, keystrokes or pointer input inside it.

What does the relay know about me?

Your Mac's device ID and name, your iPhone's device ID, the protocol version, which plan the app says you are on, and connection usage. A paid connection also sends its relay access key. Nothing else, and no account is involved.

It also sees, as any server does, the address each device connects from, and it hands each of them the other's, so they can try to reach one another directly.

Do I need an account?

No. Peering creates an identity on each device and stores it in that device's Keychain. There is nothing to sign up for and no password.

Why do the two screens show a code?

Each device creates its own key. The six digits are how you confirm that the device you are pairing with is the one in front of you. Once you confirm it, both devices remember the other's public key.

Can I transfer files?

Yes, on your Mac and on any server you add over SSH. Walk the folders, open a file, change it and save it, upload one from your phone, and save a copy down to Files.

You can also make a file or a folder, rename one, and delete one: on your own Mac that moves it to the Bin, while on a server it is gone and the confirmation says so.

What happens if my phone changes network?

The session cannot survive it: the address the Mac was sending to stops existing. The connection does not fail or close, it goes quiet, and an app that waits for an error sits on the last frame forever.

Peering counts the quiet and ends the session after ten seconds of it, so you are told. Join the new network, tap the Mac, and you are back.

Does my screen go through your servers?

Only when it has to. Your two devices try a path straight between themselves first. The relay introduces them, and carries the session when that path cannot be opened. Both are encrypted the same, and the relay reads neither.

Does it work on a network I do not control?

Yes. Once paired, your two devices find each other over the internet, so it works from a hotel, a train, or someone else's Wi-Fi.

They try a path straight between themselves first, and fall back to the relay when the network will not allow one. The local network only has to be shared for the first pairing.
