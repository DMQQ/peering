What it does

Everything below travels over one encrypted connection between your two devices.

Encrypted, device to device

Each device makes its own key and keeps it in its own Keychain. There is no account and nothing stored on a server.

Come back from anywhere

After pairing, an end-to-end encrypted relay reaches your Mac when it is on another network.

You actually drive it

Pointer, keyboard, joystick, scroll wheel and macros. Control & zoom puts your fingers on the Mac.

A screen, an app, or one window

Share any of them, and switch between them without reconnecting.

Every desktop you have

All of your Spaces, each one shown as a picture rather than a list of names.

Keeps going when you leave

Picture in Picture over other apps, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen, and a widget on the Home Screen.

The relay carries it. It cannot read it.

That is a property of how the relay is built, not a promise about how it behaves.

What the relay handles

Your devices' IDs, your Mac's name, the protocol version, which plan the app says you are on, and how much relay time has been used. That is the whole list. It is what addressing two machines and counting allowance requires.

What it cannot read

The control channel is a TLS session belonging to your two devices, copied through without being terminated. Video, audio, keystrokes, pointer input and clipboard text are sealed with keys the relay was never given.

What the app collects

Nothing. No analytics, no advertising, no third-party tracking SDKs, and no account to attach any of it to.

Questions

Can the relay see my screen or my controls?

No. It forwards the encrypted connection between your paired devices and cannot read the video, audio, clipboard text, keystrokes or pointer input inside it.

What does the relay know about me?

Your Mac's device ID and name, your iPhone's device ID, the protocol version, which plan the app says you are on, and connection usage. A paid connection also sends its relay access key. Nothing else, and no account is involved.

Do I need an account?

No. Peering creates an identity on each device and stores it in that device's Keychain. There is nothing to sign up for and no password.

Why do the two screens show a code?

Each device creates its own key. The six digits are how you confirm that the device you are pairing with is the one in front of you. Once you confirm it, both devices remember the other's public key.

Can I transfer files?

Not yet. Peering carries the screen, Mac audio, controls, terminal commands and clipboard text, but not files.

Does it work on a network I do not control?

Yes. Once paired, the connection uses the relay over the internet, so it works from a hotel, a train, or someone else's Wi-Fi. The local network only has to be shared for the first pairing.
