```

```

Commas, no spaces, no repeats of anything already in the name or the subtitle (both are indexed).

than the traffic it brings.

## Description (4000)

```
Your Mac's screen, on your iPhone.

Peering shows your Mac's display on your phone and lets you drive it: pointer, keyboard, real
right-clicks, and the apps you forgot to open. It works on your own network, and from anywhere
else through a relay.

Pair once. The two devices recognise each other from then on. No account, no sign-in, and nothing
stored on a server.

TO GET STARTED

Peering is two apps. This is the iPhone half; the Mac half is a free download from
getpeering.app. Install it on the Mac you want to reach and open it once.

Pair them on the same network, or across networks with the six-digit code the Mac shows. After
that, the connection is yours.

WHAT IT DOES

• Every display, one running app, or a single window, switchable without dropping the session
• Pointer, keyboard, scroll wheel, right-click, and a joystick for the things that want one
• On iPad, a trackpad or mouse drives the Mac's pointer: hover, click, right-click and scroll
• F1 to F12, Esc, Tab and the control keys a phone keyboard cannot type
• Pinch to zoom, fit the whole screen, or fill the phone with it
• Observe mode, when you want to watch without sending input
• Sound from the Mac, on the phone
• Picture in Picture, a Live Activity and a widget, so locking the phone does not end the session
• Every Space you have, each one drawn as a picture you can tap
• Screenshots straight to Photos
• Open an app on the Mac that was not running when you connected

PRIVATE BY CONSTRUCTION

• Straight between the two devices when the network allows it, so the picture never passes
  through a server
• End to end encrypted through the relay when it cannot
• Each device keeps its own key and refuses anything claiming to be the other
• Face ID lock, so an unlocked phone is not an unlocked Mac

KEEP AN EYE ON YOUR CODING AGENTS

Start Claude Code, Codex or a long build on your Mac and walk away. When a shell stops for you,
whether a coding agent asking for permission, a sudo password or a yes/no, Peering tells you, in
Terminal and iTerm windows as well as its own. Tap the banner and answer it from your phone.

A TERMINAL AND A FILE BROWSER TOO

• Full-screen SSH sessions with keys, passwords and port forwarding
• A file browser on the Mac and on your servers: upload, download, rename, delete, and run what
  it lists
• Clipboard, macros and quick commands

FREE, AND WHAT A PLAN ADDS

Peering is free on your own network for as long as you like: one Mac, one shell at a time, and
every display, app and window it can show. Relay, Relay Plus and Pro are for reaching your Mac
when you are away from it.

Why a subscription rather than a one-off price: away from home, your session is carried by a
relay server we run, and that server costs money every month it is on. Your own network never
touches it, which is why that part is free.

Peering for Mac is required, and is a free download at getpeering.app. Without it this app has
nothing to connect to.

Subscriptions renew automatically unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the end of the
current period, and are charged to your App Store account.

Privacy policy: https://getpeering.app/privacy
Terms of use: https://getpeering.app/terms
```

## App Review notes

**This is the field that decides whether the first submission passes**, because the app is useless
without a second app that is not on the App Store, and a reviewer works from an iPhone and an
iPad. Say what the Mac half is, where it comes from, and how to get a session running without
somebody else's Mac:

- Peering for Mac is a free download at https://getpeering.app/download. It is notarised, it is a
  developer-signed `.dmg`, and it requires macOS 15 or later.
- A session needs a Mac running it and a six-digit code read off that Mac's screen, which is the
  pairing step. Without a Mac on the network the app opens on its Connect screen and finds nothing,
  which is the correct and expected behaviour.
- If a Mac is not available to the reviewer, say so plainly and offer a screen recording of the
  pairing and a session, plus a test Mac if there is one that can be reached over the internet.
  Apple rejects "requires hardware you do not have" submissions that do not explain themselves.
